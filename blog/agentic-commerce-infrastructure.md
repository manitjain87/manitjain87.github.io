# The Agentic Commerce Infrastructure

*May 2026 · 7 min read · Strategic Finance*

---

Shopify is running 2,000 LLM-guided robot shoppers against its merchants' live storefronts. The agents browse, filter, add to cart, and abandon — simulating the full range of human purchase behaviour at machine speed. Shopify calls it SimGym. The humans who used to be A/B test subjects are no longer needed.

This is a precise description of what agentic commerce means at the infrastructure level. Not AI helping merchants sell more — AI replacing the signal sources that merchants use to understand what customers want in the first place.

That replacement is happening across every layer of the stack simultaneously.

---

## The Signal Layer

Product decisions in commerce run on behavioural signal: what users clicked, which variants converted, where sessions dropped. Human A/B testing is slow, expensive, and subject to selection effects. SimGym eliminates all three constraints. LLM-driven personas run thousands of experiments per week against live storefronts, generating signal at a cost per insight that makes traditional user research uneconomical.

DoorDash shipped a parallel version in merchant onboarding. AI reads a restaurant's website and configures its DoorDash listing automatically — 35% faster, by DoorDash's own disclosure. The signal here is the merchant's own existing data. The restaurant doesn't adapt its menu to DoorDash's format. The agent infers it from the source.

Both point at the same structural shift: the human labour cost of generating commerce signal is collapsing. The companies that own the AI that generates the signal own the distribution.

---

## The Payment Rail

For signal to produce revenue, transactions need to close. In the markets where commerce is growing fastest — Latin America, Africa, Southeast Asia — the payment infrastructure is not Stripe.

dLocal processes $41 billion in payments across 40 countries through a single API. Their merchant list includes Amazon, Microsoft, Uber, and Netflix — enterprises that chose dLocal after rigorous vetting. Revenue per employee: $3.6 million. Free cash flow conversion: 97%. Net margins: 18% — not software margins, but a genuine cash machine at 1% penetration of a $3.6 trillion TAM growing 25–35% annually.

---

## The Scale Proof

MercadoLibre is what this looks like when it works.

Q1 2026: revenue +49% year-over-year — the fastest growth since Q2 2022. Logistics cost per shipment in Brazil fell 17% in the same quarter that items sold rose 56%. LLM search deployed across Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina with visible funnel impact on purchase conversion. 2.7 million new credit cards issued in a single quarter.

This is not a company deploying AI for efficiency. It is a company whose AI-native logistics and search infrastructure is the moat itself. The flywheel is self-reinforcing: more volume → lower cost per shipment → lower prices → more volume. The 17% logistics cost reduction did not come from a cost programme. It came from scale. Competitors cannot replicate 42 fulfillment centers and 50%+ same or next-day delivery without five years and $20 billion in capital.

That is the operating model of an AI-native commerce infrastructure company at $85 billion in market cap.

---

## The Honest Take

When Adam Foroughi rebuilt AppLovin, he cut the core business to about 400 people. As he described it: "It's reaching or over $10 million a head" in EBIT per employee. The product — an AI recommendation model running billions of ad auctions per day — generates more value per person than almost any business built before it. "We don't have a product organization," Foroughi said. "Our engineers are meant to be product managers." That is not a management theory. It is the operating model of an AI-native company: one team builds the model, the model does the work at scale.

The commerce infrastructure equivalent is being assembled now. Shopify owns the storefront layer and is building agent-native tooling — SimGym is the leading indicator of what that looks like. dLocal owns the payment rail for the markets Stripe doesn't reach — 40 countries, single API, 97% cash conversion. MercadoLibre has proven the unit economics work at scale: 42 fulfillment centers, 50%+ same-day delivery, LLM search across three markets improving purchase conversion.

The UCP consortium — Google, Shopify, Amazon, Visa, Mastercard, Stripe — is finalising the credential standards for how agents query merchants and transact. That is the authentication layer. The commerce layer — the actual payments, the fulfillment, the logistics — is being assembled by the companies that have spent years building physical infrastructure in the markets growing fastest.

The window to build against these rails is not measured in years. The businesses that will process agent-to-merchant commerce in Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia in 2030 are being chosen now — not by consumers, but by the infrastructure decisions their enterprises are making today.
