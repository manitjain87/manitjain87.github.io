Inference got a hundred times cheaper this year. The compute bill went up anyway.

That observation — posted by an inference engineer at Nebius — captures the most important thing happening in AI capital allocation right now. JPMorgan just flagged Shopify, Spotify, Meta, and Pinterest as companies where AI token costs are eating profits alive. Goldman Sachs published the optimistic version the same week: falling token costs will convert AI from a capex burden into margin expansion. Both are right. The question is which side of that gap your company is on.

Three things every CFO should understand:

1. GPU compute is physical infrastructure, not a software line item. Nebius' founder describes it directly: "We build it through the whole stack. Land, power, racks, cloud platform. It is optimized to the last bolt." The CapEx required — $16–20 billion per year — is utility-scale. Demand is growing 100%+ annually.

2. "Whatever is built is consumed." Every major GPU provider is sold out. The constraint is not chip supply — it's the physical speed at which data centers can be built.

3. The shift from seats to consumption pricing is accelerating. Bare metal → tokens → agentic services. The $27 billion Meta contract is not the business. It's the balance sheet that enables it.

This is not a Nebius stock recommendation. It's a capital allocation framework for the physical infrastructure layer of AI.
