Systems Thinking

The flywheel that powers everything I build.

Four systems, one reinforcing loop. Gyanchand ingests and classifies data from hundreds of sources into a structured research database. Charlie synthesises that intelligence into investment analysis using frameworks from Buffett, Munger, Thiel, and Druckenmiller. Chokepoint scans for bottleneck patterns — physical constraints that multiple spending streams must flow through. Ai, Honestly distributes the sharpest signals as published content, generating feedback that sharpens future research.

The same architecture pattern applies to finance integration: data ingestion from multiple systems → consolidation and normalisation → FP&A analysis and scenario modelling → management reporting → actuals-vs-forecast feedback that improves the next cycle. This is not a technology story. It is a systems design story — and it is how I think about every finance function I build.

Mental Models

Thinking Frameworks

Post-Merger Integration
Map the full stack before you touch anything. Standardise where possible, preserve what works. The integration is not the goal — the operating model is.
Marketplace Economics
Five tenets: merchant-first, healthy unit economics, customer experience, reinvestment, trust. Every marketplace breaks when one of these is neglected.
AI-Native Org Design
SaaS seats become variable AI consumption costs. Headcount decouples from output. The finance function rebuilds around data infrastructure, not spreadsheets.
Turnaround Finance
People-centered transformation. With the right focus, accountability, and data, talented teams deliver results that forecasting models cannot predict.
Capital Allocation
Every dollar has an opportunity cost. The job is not to say no — it is to ensure every yes is the best possible use of scarce capital. Incentives drive outcomes.
Systems Architecture
Finance is infrastructure. Map the full stack. Understand which systems talk to which. Build the architecture before you automate the processes.
